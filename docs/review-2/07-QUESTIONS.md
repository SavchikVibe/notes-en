# 07 — The seven questions

Answer these directly. Each answer should be a judgement with evidence, not a feature list. If
the honest answer is “cannot tell from the packet”, say what you would need.

## 1. Is the A1 → B2 → C1 progression real, or are there gaps at the seams?

Look at the six sample units and the level tables. What can a learner actually do at the end of
A1, A2, B1, B2, C1? Where does the course jump? Is the C1 bridge (24 units + 8 projects) enough
to call it C1, or is it “strong B2 plus academic topics”? What is missing at each seam: grammar,
vocabulary, discourse, reading stamina, writing, speaking?

## 2. Is 12–14 exercises enough to mark a unit complete?

The unit loop is mostly recognition and controlled production. Translation and assemble require
production of a sentence, but the sentence is prompted in Russian and scaffolded. There is no
free writing in the 234 units. Is “unit done” a meaningful claim? If not, what is the cheapest
change that makes it meaningful without turning a 10–15-minute lesson into a 40-minute one?
Consider: a short free-production step, a spoken summary, a typed answer checked by a human, a
portfolio requirement, or a different definition of “done”.

## 3. Does the app test understanding, or memory of formulations?

The reading questions and evidence quotes are meant to test comprehension. The library adds
True/False/Not Given and matching headings. The unit exercises often reuse a word's example
sentence. Where is the line between retrieval practice and “the learner remembers the sentence,
not the language”? Give concrete examples from the samples and say what to change.

## 4. Is the feedback enough to learn from?

Current feedback:

- choice/listen: correct option highlighted, short explanation sometimes;
- assemble/translate: “Верно/Неверно” plus the expected answer; no diff;
- reading: correct option plus evidence quote;
- writing: checklist + model answer, self-assessed;
- speaking: recording + checklist, self-assessed;
- errors: return later, but no explanation beyond the exercise.

What is the cheapest change with the largest effect on learning? Be specific: diff for
translate/assemble, “why this option is wrong”, a model answer per unit, a revision prompt, a
second attempt rule, or something else.

## 5. Is the workload sustainable for a 9th-grader doing 2 hours a day?

A 120-minute day: 45 course (3–4 units), 25 listening, 30 reading, 10 speaking, 10 SRS. Plus
homework and school. A Studio task takes 15–40 minutes and is not in the daily split. Where will
fatigue, boredom or burnout come from? What should be cut, reordered or made optional? Is the
90-minute tutor day realistic? Would a 5-day week with one lighter day work better?

## 6. What breaks first when the course grows from 234 to ~400 units?

Consider the single 2.6 MB bundle, the monolithic content imports, the validator, the answer
skew, the review queue, the path screen, the session builder, the tests. What is the cheapest
structural fix now, before the course doubles? Be concrete about the failure mode and the cost.

## 7. What are the top three changes for the next two weeks, and what should not be done?

Assume one agent and one human, about 2 weeks, no new content authoring beyond what is needed.
What would raise the score by one point? What looks tempting but would hurt the product? Include
effort estimates (S/M/L) and say what to measure afterwards to know it worked.
